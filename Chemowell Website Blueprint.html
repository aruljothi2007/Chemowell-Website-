Chemowell

A daily treatment tracker for people having chemotherapy at home. Built to be simple, clear and safe, because patients often face severe fatigue, "chemo brain" and anxiety.

This blueprint sets out the structure, the daily metrics and the design rules for the platform.

Sample daily check-in

Recommended website architecture

Two distinct experiences: an ultra-simple dashboard for patients and a trend-focused portal for caregivers and oncologists.

Landing page

Public-facing entry point with login, assurances about secure authentication, and onboarding instructions for new patients.

Patient dashboard

The main screen. A quick daily symptom check-in, medication reminders and simple visual progress trends.

Caregiver / doctor portal

A separate interface for secure, permission-based viewing of data, trend analysis and automated threshold alerts.

Resources & emergency hub

Easy-to-find documentation and a prominent one-click way to contact the on-call oncology nurse.

Core data to track daily

Forms use large buttons, sliders and visual scales instead of typing.

CategoryMetricsWhy it matters
Vital signsBody temperature, weight, blood pressureFever is a critical emergency for neutropenic patients (low white blood cells). A sudden drop in weight can point to dehydration or malnutrition.
Symptom severityNausea, fatigue, pain, diarrhea, constipationShows how well treatment is tolerated and whether supportive care medications are working.
MedicationOral chemo doses, steroids, anti-nausea pillsKeeps complex medication schedules and chemotherapy cycles on track.
HydrationFluid intake (glasses of water), meals eatenDehydration is a leading cause of emergency hospital readmission during active chemotherapy.
WellbeingMood, anxiety level, sleep quality and durationTracks the psychological and physical toll of chemotherapy on sleep and emotional health.

Design and UX principles

Chemo-brain friendly layout

Massive touch-friendly buttons, a single vertical column, and one simple question per screen to avoid overload.

Smart color coding

Soft, calming colors for daily logging. Deep crimson is reserved for danger signs only.

Lavender Pale blue Pastel green Danger only

One-touch emergency button

A persistent, highly visible "Call My Care Team" button on every view. It is pinned to the bottom of this page as an example.

Offline functionality

Service worker caching lets patients who are traveling or have spotty internet save entries on the device and sync when they reconnect.

Technical implementation ideas

No-code / low-code prototype

Use a rapid tool such as Bubble or Glide with an Airtable backend to build a working, responsive MVP with user login.

HIPAA / GDPR standards

This is highly private health data, so use end-to-end encryption and secure platforms such as Supabase or AWS.
